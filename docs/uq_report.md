## Known limitations

- Results are on simulated data only, with no real-world validation cells.
- There is a small number of runs, so run-level intervals are wide.
- All legacy results use the post-step mask, which is derived from the predicted step. The new Baseline uses the causal `mask_in`.
- Melt-pool functionals use each sample's own T_l. Depth is capped by the domain height.
- Melt-pool volume is capped by the patch: a pool wider than the 64-voxel patch is truncated, so large volumes are lower bounds. The patch-limited stratum shows how often this happens.
- Temperatures are stored as float16, which limits T_max resolution at high temperatures.
- The solver has no evaporation, recoil pressure, or keyhole physics. Above the boiling point its temperatures are unphysical (up to about 17 000 K); the keyhole regime marks those samples as outside its validity.
- Boiling points of alloys are model values from LPBF simulation literature, not sharp measured points.
- The solver grid is 2 um; grid convergence against 1 um is not checked, so intervals hold against this solver, not against the physics.
- Material data share one absorptivity (0.4) for every alloy; the Ti64 cp table dips between 300 and 373 K.
- No physics loss: the available PDE residual does not yet match the solver (fixed dt, scalar k).
